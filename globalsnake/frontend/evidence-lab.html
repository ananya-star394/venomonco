REPRODUCIBLE EVIDENCE REVIEW

Evidence Lab

Standardise claim-level annotations, inspect an explainable evidence-support score, and compare candidates without turning missing information into a negative biological conclusion.

Important: this is a transparent, rule-based review aid—not a validated statistical model, clinical prediction, safety assessment, or measure of therapeutic efficacy. Scores reflect only the evidence features entered by the reviewer. Missing evidence earns no support points; it does not prove inactivity.

Suggested display bands (project-defined, not validated): 0–24 limited documented support; 25–49 preliminary support; 50–74 moderate documented support; 75–100 stronger documented support. The score does not assess clinical readiness, efficacy, or safety.

4 · Curation protocol

  1. Find the primary paper or primary database record; record DOI/PMID and the date checked.
  2. Verify that the peptide identity and venom/source attribution match the claim.
  3. Capture each experimental result separately: cancer type, cell line, assay, endpoint, value and units, exposure time, controls, and replicate details.
  4. Label mechanism, selectivity, toxicity, and structure evidence independently. A PDB match is not evidence of anticancer activity.
  5. Record contradictions and missing fields explicitly. Do not convert “not reported” into “inactive”, “safe”, or “no research”.
  6. Have a second reviewer check high-priority claims when possible; retain reviewer notes and source links.

This page is a prototype curation aid. For production deployment, persist records with server-side authentication, audit history, reviewer identifiers, versioning, and validated access controls.
